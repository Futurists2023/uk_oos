Executive Summary & Value PropositionConsumer product brands in the Small-to-Medium Enterprise (SME) bracket face a structural blind spot in wholesale distribution: once inventory is delivered to independent retail stockists (boutiques, specialty stores, independent pharmacies), the brand loses operational visibility.When an independent retailer sells out of a product, reordering is slow, inconsistent, and manual. Often, store owners forget to reorder, or automated store management tools automatically unpublish sold-out items from their storefronts, hiding stockouts from brand representatives entirely. Furthermore, retailers frequently fail to order a brand's top-performing hero products, settling for fragmented, low-margin assortments.This platform operates as an automated Wholesale Revenue Intelligence Engine. It continuously audits public retail storefronts across independent merchant networks to detect empty shelves and assortment gaps, alerting SME wholesale teams to immediate replenishment and upsell opportunities.2. Market Problem & Industry DynamicsThe Wholesale Blind SpotLarge enterprise brands use Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) systems and vendor-managed inventory agreements to monitor store-level stock in major retail chains. SME brands (£500k–£15m annual turnover) selling to independent retail networks cannot afford or enforce enterprise EDI.Consequently, SME wholesale operations suffer from two silent revenue drains:Unrealized Replenishment Revenue (Stockouts):
Independent retailers do not systematically trigger purchase orders when stock depletes. An empty shelf can sit unoccupied for 30 to 90 days before a store buyer notices or a travelling sales rep visits.Catalog Fragmentation & Silent Stockouts:
Many independent merchants use automated inventory rules that unpublish or hide products the moment local stock reaches zero to avoid cluttering their customer-facing catalog. To the brand, it appears the store simply does not stock that item, masking an active stockout as an absent listing.Assortment Inefficiency:
Retail buyers often order an idiosyncratic selection of niche or trial-size SKUs while neglecting the brand's verified top-selling revenue generators.3. They do not want speculative marketing leads or cold outreach lists; they want direct, frictionless reasons to invoice active trade accounts that already have open credit terms and approved vendor records.4. Signal 1: Out-of-Stock (OOS) DetectionTrigger: An active retail stockist has an SKU listed, but inventory status indicates it is unavailable for immediate consumer purchase.Commercial Implication: Immediate loss of sales. Every day the item remains unstocked represents lost reorder value for the brand and risks the consumer defecting to an alternative brand.Sales Action: The brand's sales rep contacts the retail buyer with an existing stockist re-order draft: "You've been out of stock on our 50ml SKU for 5 days; our warehouse can dispatch replenishment on Net-30 terms tomorrow."Signal 2: Missing Hero SKU (Assortment Gap)Trigger: An independent retailer stocks 1 or more SKUs from a brand, but does not carry the brand’s verified, high-velocity "Hero Product" (the flagship item carried by the vast majority of peer retailers in that sector).Commercial Implication: The retailer has an active trade account and customer footfall for the brand, but is under-monetizing the shelf space by stocking secondary or low-velocity SKUs.Sales Action: Low-risk upsell backed by peer validation: "You currently carry our body wash and scrub, but 85% of independent boutiques in the UK generate their primary revenue from our Daily Facial Cream. Let's add a half-case to your next delivery."5. System Methodology & Pipeline LogicThe system operates across three discrete logical phases: Curation, Expansion, and Audit. Phase 1: Brand Curation via "The Intersection Rule"Rather than pulling unvetted brand directories from wholesale aggregators (which are full of inactive hobbyists with zero real retail distribution), the system uses a cluster of established, high-reputation multi-brand independent retailers as Seed Nodes.Extraction: Ingest all brand names and catalog offerings across a curated cluster of premier multi-brand retail businesses.The Intersection Filter: Calculate brand frequency across the seed cluster.If a brand appears in only 1 store: Discarded as an exclusive arrangement or low-volume micro-brand.If a brand appears in 2 or more independent stores: Qualified as a commercially viable SME brand with proven wholesale distribution, physical manufacturing capacity, and dedicated retail accounts.Phase 2: Network Expansion (Stockist Mapping)Once the master roster of high-velocity SME brands is established, the platform identifies the broader universe of independent stockists across the market:Co-Occurrence Discovery: Instead of searching for single brands (which leads to single-store saturation), the engine pairs competing or complementary qualified SME brands together.Identification: Websites carrying two or more non-competing independent brands simultaneously are confirmed as multi-brand independent retail stockists.Registry: Discovered domains are added to a centralized stockist directory, categorized by vertical.Phase 3: The Audit Engine & Baseline ModelingWith the brand roster and stockist network established, the system executes continuous monitoring:Catalog Ingestion: For each stockist, map all brand-associated products and their variant-level availability states.Hero SKU Baseline Calculation: Across the entire network of stores stocking a brand, calculate the distribution percentage of each individual product. Any SKU present in $\ge 70\%$ of active stockists is statistically tagged as the brand's Hero SKU.Signal Evaluation:Flag any variant marked as unavailable as an OOS Signal.Compare each store's active brand catalog against the brand's global Hero SKUs. If a store carries the brand but omits the flagship item, flag as an Assortment Gap Signal.6. Commercial Strategy & MonetizationThe "Proof of Leak" Teaser (Cold Acquisition):Prospecting brands are sent a verified, zero-cost sample audit of 3 of their actual independent stockists currently out of stock or missing hero items.The hook is undeniable: the proof of lost money is already sitting in front of them.Subscription Tiering (DaaS Model):Tier 1 (Core Monitoring): Monthly audit of up to 50 designated retail stockists; weekly replenishment dispatches.Tier 2 (Continuous Surveillance): Weekly automated audits across the entire national network (both known stockists and newly discovered independent retailers); real-time stockout alerts.Enterprise / Multi-Brand Portfolio: Custom monitoring for brand aggregators and wholesale distributor agencies managing 5+ subsidiary brands.8. Defensibility & Strategic BoundariesNo Price Enforcement (Anti-MAP): The system strictly avoids price surveillance or minimum advertised price (MAP) tracking. Price policing creates adversarial friction between retailers and brands. Our product positioning is strictly positive-sum: driving more re-orders and higher retail sell-through.No Speculative Lead Lists: The system does not sell random directories of unconnected stores. Every signal is anchored to an active customer relationship or an existing category-level fit.Operational Simplicity: The business is built on public storefront accessibility and structural distribution patterns, ensuring zero reliance on private brand credentials, store integrations, or manual retailer cooperation.
